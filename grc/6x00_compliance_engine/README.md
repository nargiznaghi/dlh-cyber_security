# The Compliance Engine

> *"Measure what is measurable, and make measurable what is not so."* — Galileo Galilei

## Introduction
You have spent weeks building endpoint baselines, Sigma detection rules, IR playbooks, and forensic runbooks. But if an auditor walked in tomorrow asking you to prove MedDefense has an adequate security program, could you? Compliance is the discipline of measuring actual controls against recognized standards, identifying gaps, enforcing policy, and producing evidence an independent auditor can evaluate without verbal explanation.

## Why This Matters
Security controls that exist technically but lack governance documentation do not survive an audit. Auditors operate strictly on evidence, not trust. Mastering gap assessments, policy drafting, vendor risk reviews, and compliance briefings bridges technical execution with executive governance—a core capability for GRC analysts, security program managers, and CISOs.

## Context
Four weeks after incident `IR-2026-0420-01`, Dr. Patricia Morales receives an HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR) desk audit notice covering 15 HIPAA categories. MedDefense has six weeks to prepare. Key organizational gaps exist:
- **No Formal Assessment:** MedDefense has never benchmarked against NIST CSF 2.0 or HIPAA.
- **Outdated Policies:** Acceptable Use, Data Classification, and Vendor Management policies are missing or unreviewed since 2021.
- **Third-Party Risk:** CloudVault Medical (clinical backup provider) lacks a security assessment and an executed Business Associate Agreement (BAA).

Your goal: map existing controls, write enforceable policies, evaluate vendor risk, and deliver an auditable compliance package.
