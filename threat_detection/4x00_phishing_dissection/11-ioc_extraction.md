## IOC Quality & Safety Assessment

Understanding IOC fidelity is critical to prevent false positives and unnecessary operational disruption:

1. **High-Confidence IOCs (Safe to Block Immediately):**
   - **Lookalike Domains (`meddefense-portal[.]com`, `meddefense-benefits[.]org`, etc.):** High fidelity. MedDefense owns no legitimate infrastructure under these domains. Blocking at firewall, proxy, and DNS levels carries zero risk of false positives.
   - **Specific Phishing URLs and Email Addresses:** Extremely precise indicators. Immediate perimeter blocking and retroactive email purge are strongly recommended.
   - **Attachment Hash (`e3b0c44298fc1c149afbf4c8996fb92427ae41e4649b934ca495991b7852b855`):** Unique identifier for the malicious PDF invoice artifact. Safe to block across endpoint (EDR) and email security solutions.

2. **Medium-Confidence IOCs (Monitor or Alert-Only):**
   - **Dedicated Originating IPs (`91.234.99.107`, `51.38.42.17`, `185.176.43.22`, `164.90.218.73`):** High confidence for current blocking, but IPs on cloud/VPS providers can be reassigned over time. Recommend blocking with a 30-to-90-day review cycle.
   - **`PHPMailer 6.6.0` and `PHP-{hex}` Message-ID Formats:** Useful for mail gateway rules and threat hunting, but legitimate web services occasionally use PHPMailer. These signatures should trigger elevated spam/phishing scoring rather than hard drop actions on their own.

3. **Low-Confidence / Context-Only Indicators (Do NOT Block Alone):**
   - **Hosting Provider Infrastructure / IP Ranges:** Blocking whole hosting provider ASNs (e.g., DigitalOcean or OVH) will cause significant false positives and break legitimate third-party service connections.
   - **Generic Keyword Hostnames (`portal`, `benefits`):** Must never be blocked as standalone strings, as legitimate corporate services rely on these naming conventions.
